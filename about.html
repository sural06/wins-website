About

Our mission

Stanford Women in National Security prepares women to lead in defense, intelligence, and foreign policy — through mentorship, direct access to practitioners, and a community that takes both ambition and rigor seriously.

[Placeholder history — edit freely.] Stanford Women in National Security was founded by a small group of students who noticed how few women were represented in the campus's national security and foreign policy pipeline — in classes, in speaker line-ups, and in the internships that lead to the field. The organization started as a handful of informal dinners with visiting practitioners and has grown into a standing community with a mentorship structure, a briefing series, and relationships with partner organizations in Washington and the Bay Area.

What we stand for

  • Rigor first

    We treat national security as a discipline, not a talking point. Briefings and discussions are expected to go past headlines into how decisions actually get made.

  • Access, not just inspiration

    A speaker series is only useful if it opens doors. We prioritize relationships that turn into internships, informational interviews, and letters of recommendation — not just a photo after the talk.

  • Built by women, open to everyone

    The organization exists because women have been underrepresented in this field. General meetings and briefings are open to any Stanford student who takes the subject seriously.
